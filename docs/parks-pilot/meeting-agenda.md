## Agenda — 60 min

### 1. Open (5 min) — read the room
- Brief greeting; ask how their week is going. Don't launch into pitch.
- "Before we get into this, anything I should know about how Parks usually structures inbound partnerships? I want to fit how you already work, not propose something out of left field."
- Listen. This tells you what to emphasize/de-emphasize.

### 2. Why FALCO exists (8 min) — the wedge
- Brief story: distressed Tennessee homeowners are losing six figures of equity per family because the only people calling them are wholesalers.
- Show site briefly on phone or laptop: the homepage, the manifesto math section.
- Land the point: "We built the data pipeline and the homeowner conversation. We need a licensed auction firm to run the actual sale. That's why I'm here."
- **Don't oversell the data pipeline.** Acknowledge it's still ramping. The point is the OUTREACH and the EDUCATION machinery are real and ready.

### 3. What Parks already does well (5 min) — flatter honestly
- "You guys have 40+ years of TN auction work. You have the brand, the licenses, the marketing infrastructure, the auctioneer reputation. None of that I can build in 12 months. None of it I want to."
- "What I want is to feed you a steady flow of qualified inventory in a segment you haven't been actively pursuing — distressed pre-foreclosure single-family — and let you do what you already do well."

### 4. Hand him the term sheet (2 min)
- "I drafted a term sheet for what I'm thinking. It's intentionally small — 3 deals, 3 months. I want both of us to prove this works on real properties before either of us makes a bigger commitment. Take a look."
- Slide it across. Stay quiet. Let him read.

### 5. Walk through the term sheet (15 min) — section by section
- **Sections 1-2** (purpose + scope): "I want this small enough that it's a low-risk yes for you and structured enough that we can both measure whether it's working."
- **Section 3** (what FALCO delivers): walk through quickly. Emphasize the math sheet — pull out the printed sample. "This is what the homeowner sees BEFORE they commit. They show up to your listing call already educated."
- **Section 4** (what Parks delivers): "Tell me if I got anything wrong about how you typically run a campaign. I tried to write what I assumed your standard process is — happy to defer to your way of doing it."
- **Section 5** (economics): walk through the 8% premium → 5/3 split. **Show the printed economics one-pager.** Walk through the per-deal math on a $484K Davidson home.
  - "On that one deal — Parks earns ~$21,500, FALCO earns ~$13,000, homeowner nets ~$95-130K instead of $25K from a wholesaler. Three deals like that is $64,500 to Parks and $39,000 to me, plus three Tennessee families that don't lose their equity."
- **Section 6** (success metrics): "I want a 30-min review at month 3 with both of us looking at the actual numbers. If it worked, we talk about formalizing. If it didn't, we shake hands and move on."
- **Section 7-12**: skim. Note that termination is 14 days for either party — neither of us is locked in.

### 6. The questions YOU need answered (10 min)
Don't leave without these:

1. **"What's your current monthly listing capacity?"** — tells you how many deals he can absorb if FALCO scales.
2. **"What's a typical campaign length for residential auction at Parks?"** — confirms the 30-60 day model.
3. **"What's your historical clearance percentage on TN distressed marketed sales?"** — reality-check on the 80-88% target.
4. **"Are you currently taking distressed inventory from anyone else? Any conflicts I should know about?"** — surface dealbreakers early.
5. **"Who at Parks would I be coordinating with day-to-day if we get to the first listing?"** — find out if it's Dale or someone on his team.
6. **"What's your minimum deal size to be worth running a campaign?"** — figure out which inventory NOT to send.
7. **"What's your preferred handoff format — email, phone, shared doc?"** — operational fit.

### 7. Listen for his asks (10 min)
- He WILL have things he wants. Don't preempt — let him ask.
- Common ones to expect: marketing co-op for first deal, slightly tilted economics, a faster acceptance window than 5 days, exclusivity in his counties (Rutherford, Bedford), brand mention on FALCO site.
- **What you can give:** longer pilot, brand mention on /partners after first close, shared marketing on first 1-2 deals to lower his risk.
- **What you should NOT give:** above 5% to Parks, exclusivity in this pilot (locks you into one partner before you know the market).

### 8. Close (5 min) — get the verbal commit
- "So — does this give you enough to move forward? Anything I haven't addressed?"
- If yes: "Great. I'll send you a clean version reflecting today's discussion within 48 hours. Sound good?"
- If maybe: "What would you need to see to be a yes?"
- If no: "Got it. What's the dealbreaker?"
- **Schedule the next touch before you leave.** "Can I follow up by phone Friday at noon?"

## Don't do

- Don't claim FALCO has done deals it hasn't. Be clear this is a pilot.
- Don't promise volume you can't deliver. The term sheet says "best effort" for a reason.
- Don't trash competitors by name (auction firms, brokers, even wholesalers). Talk about the system, not the people.
- Don't agree to anything material in the room. "Let me think through that and come back to you tomorrow" is a complete sentence and shows you're disciplined.
- Don't oversell the tokenization angle. Don't bring it up unless he asks. It's not relevant to this pilot.
- Don't tell him your uncle is investing. That's between you and your investors. He doesn't need to know.
